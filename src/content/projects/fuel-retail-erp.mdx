## Overview

I built and run this platform for an oil-company-branded fuel retail outlet. The current version
started with a business requirements document and a software requirements specification I wrote
with the owner in early October 2026, and went to production on 21 October. The code repository is private;
I am happy to walk through it in an interview.

## What it covers

- **Shifts and duties**: attendants are assigned to nozzles; every meter movement belongs to whoever
  holds that nozzle.
- **Hand-overs**: at each hand-over the meters are read, and the movement since the last reading is
  that attendant's to account for, in cash, digital payments and credit slips.
- **Tankers and wet stock**: tanker receipts by compartment, tank dips with calibration charts, and
  a daily stock check that compares book stock against physical stock within configurable
  tolerances.
- **Daily business report**: one document per day that the owner reviews and acknowledges, built
  bottom-up from meter readings, dips and settlements.
- **Telegram notifications and points**: the owner and supervisors get events where they already
  are, and attendants earn points for punctuality against the rota.

## Engineering decisions that mattered

### Corrections without editing history

Submitted records are never edited. Until the day's report is submitted, a supervisor or the owner
can correct a shift, a hand-over or a dip, always with a reason. The correction **cancels the
original, reverses its postings and submits an amended copy in one transaction**, and records the
change in an edit log the owner sees. After the report is submitted, nothing is correctable.

### Event-driven business days

The first version tied shifts to the clock. Real stations do not work that way: if fuel runs out at
5 PM the business closes at 5 PM; on a busy night it closes the next morning. I wrote a change request,
now with the owner for approval, that remodels the day as a *business run* that opens and closes on
events, with times as reminders that never block.

### Releases with real money involved

- Every merge to `master` runs the test suite, deploys to a staging bench with practice data, and
  smoke-tests it.
- Production only receives a version tag on `master`, verified against the app version, tested
  again, and **deployed only by a deliberate manual dispatch from an allowed deployer**, with a
  backup taken first.
- New behavior ships behind a setting that is off by default. Changes to the money model use
  expand-contract: new records are written next to the old ones, compared, then switched over.

### Tests at three levels

About 420 Python test functions cover services and APIs, Playwright suites cover station and pump
flows end to end including visual checks, and smoke tests run after every deploy.

## My role

Everything: requirements with the owner, data model, backend, the station and pump apps, CI/CD,
server set-up and production support. I used AI coding agents heavily and wrote the agent
guidelines (`AGENTS.md`) that keep their output consistent with the codebase and its tests.
